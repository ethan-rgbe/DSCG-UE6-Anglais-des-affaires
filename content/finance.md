## Overview

The **Finance** theme covers how businesses, banks and financial markets interact: how companies raise money, how markets price risk, and what happens when the system comes under stress.

Three areas come up repeatedly in DSCG UE6 orals:

- **Banks and financial intermediation** — commercial banks, investment banks, and their role in channelling savings towards investment.
- **Financial markets and instruments** — equities, bonds, derivatives, and the exchanges and indices that price them.
- **Financial crises** — recurring episodes (the 2008 subprime crisis is the standard reference point) where credit dries up, asset prices collapse, and central banks intervene.

## Key ideas to be ready to discuss

**Financing instruments.** A company can raise capital either by *issuing equity* (selling shares, diluting ownership) or by *issuing debt* (bonds, bank loans, which must be repaid with interest but do not dilute control). Exam questions often ask candidates to weigh the trade-offs between the two.

**Market regulation.** Since 2008, regulators have pushed banks towards holding more capital (Basel III) and towards greater transparency, to reduce the risk of another systemic crisis.

**Central banks.** Institutions such as the Federal Reserve or the European Central Bank set interest rates and act as "lender of last resort" during a credit crunch — a useful link back to this theme's core vocabulary.

## Talking points for the interview

- Be ready to explain a financial term in your own words rather than just translating it.
- Have one concrete example ready (a company, a crisis, a policy) for each sub-topic — examiners respond well to candidates who go beyond the textbook definition.
- Recent articles on interest-rate decisions or bank earnings are a natural bridge between this theme and the *Actualité* tab.
